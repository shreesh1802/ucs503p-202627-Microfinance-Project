    W3: Testing the repay flow end to end, two data bugs, and fixing a noisy score

    Starting point

    The payments feature from W1 (wallet balance, live repayment, score recompute in the same commit) had only been checked once, by hand. Before building on it I ran it end to end properly: through the API with error cases, then through the real UI in a browser, against a backed-up copy of the database so the test payments could be undone afterwards.

    What the end-to-end test confirmed

    Via the API (borrower 424): a payment moved the wallet by exactly the EMI, cut the loan's outstanding balance, advanced the cycle (EMI #6 -> #7), and stored a new score row. The error paths all behaved: paying someone else's loan -> 403, viewing another borrower's due summary -> 403, nonexistent loan -> 404, wallet too small -> 400 with the balance and the amount due, no token -> 401. In the UI I logged in, went through the demo payment-gateway sign-in, paid a loan, and checked the wallet, the "Paid ... Late" banner, the new score and the database rows all agreed. No console errors apart from the app's normal logged-out /auth/me 401s.

    Bug found: a loan with nothing owed was still payable

    Loan 619 had a 0.00 outstanding balance but was still Active, and the Repay page offered to charge a full EMI for it. Root cause was in the synthetic data generator, not the payment code: it tracks the balance as principal only (principal - paid_total) while every EMI includes interest, so loans hit zero before their last cycle -- and the generator only closed a loan if it reached zero and its final cycle. 64 loans were affected.

    Fix: the generator now closes any loan whose balance reaches zero; a one-off idempotent migration (migrate_close_paid_loans.py) closed the 64 existing ones (second run closed 0); and the payment service skips loans with nothing owed and closes a loan when its balance reaches zero.

    Bug found: a missed cycle stayed "due" forever

    The cycle number was derived by counting only On_Time and Late events, so a Missed or Partial event never moved the pointer. Loan 620's Missed 2026-08-21 row sat next to a computed due date of 2026-05-23, and the cycle that had already been recorded as missed could never be cleared. There were 2,071 Missed and 1,583 Partial events in the data.

    Fix: every recorded event now resolves its cycle, so the pointer advances past a Missed or Partial cycle. Loan 620 went from EMI #14 to

    18. I updated the formula in API_CONTRACT_PAYMENTS.md to match. One

    consequence worth knowing: Active loans whose events already cover their whole tenure, but which still carry a balance (196 of them), no longer show as payable -- a missed cycle is treated as written off, not as arrears to catch up. Making arrears payable would be a separate design decision.

    Re-ran the whole flow in the UI afterwards: Household (619) gone from the list, Small business at EMI #18, payment took the wallet down by exactly the EMI, database rows consistent.

    Investigation: the score went down (and up) after late payments

    While testing I noticed a borrower's score moved in ways that didn't match the payment: 852 -> 848 after one late payment, then 848 -> 860 after another, including one 179 days overdue.

    I tested it counterfactually without touching the database -- recompute the score for the same borrower with one extra On_Time, Late or Missed event added. For borrower 424, an On_Time payment, a Late one at 5 days and a Late one at 179 days all gave the same -5. Across 271 SHG-linked borrowers the average direction was right (On_Time +4.9, Late -3.1, Missed -31), but per borrower it was unreliable: 19% saw an On_Time payment lower their score, 17-21% saw Late score above On_Time.

    Two causes:

    1. No monotonicity. The XGBoost model was unconstrained and trained on only 474 people, so it fit noise: raising late_rate from 0.18 to 0.25 raised one borrower's score (847 -> 864), and 200 average days late scored above 100.
    2. n_repayment_events as a feature. It measures how long someone's history is, not how well they repay, and every payment of any kind bumps it by one -- one extra event moved a score by 5 points and the feature swung it by up to 87 points over a narrow range.

    I also realised part of the 852 -> 842 I'd seen was my own loan-closing migration changing n_closed_loans for 64 borrowers, leaving stored scores stale until the next scoring run.

    Fix: monotone constraints, drop the length feature, retrain, rescore

    Before changing the real model I compared variants in a scratch script using 5-fold cross-validation, because a single 95-person holdout is too small to trust: unconstrained 0.915 AUC, monotone 0.913, monotone without n_repayment_events 0.916 -- accuracy unchanged, behaviour much better.

    Applied it: n_repayment_events removed from the model's feature list (still computed for the cold-start blend and anomaly check), monotone constraints added in train_scoring_model.py (more on-time/savings/ attendance/peer repayment can only lower default risk; more late/partial/ missed or days late can only raise it), model retrained, then score_all re-scored all 1,079 borrowers (history is kept -- new rows are appended).

    Final run: everything together

    After all four fixes (loan closing, missed cycles, scoring, lender names) were in, I ran the whole repay flow once more from a backed-up database and restored it afterwards. Borrower 424 started at wallet Rs. 14,272.50.

    Through the API: the due summary now listed two loans (Household 619 gone), each with a lender name ("Rural Trust NBFC") and the Small business loan at EMI #18 of 24. Paying it moved the wallet to 13,198.09 -- exactly the EMI -- recorded a Late payment (59 days) and lowered the score 858 -> 849, the direction the new model is supposed to move. Every error case still behaved: paying the already-closed loan 619 -> 400, someone else's loan or due summary -> 403, unknown loan -> 404, wallet too small (borrower 1: 774.71 against 1,293.50 due) -> 400 insufficient_funds, no token -> 401.

    Through the real UI: logged in as the borrower, opened Repay, signed into the mock gateway, and the page already reflected the API payment (wallet 13,198.09, Small business at EMI #19). Each row now read "Rural Trust NBFC · Purpose" instead of a blank lender. Paying the Agriculture loan showed "Paid Rs. 2,052.66 -- Late", moved the wallet to 11,145.43 (13,198.09 - 2,052.66) and advanced the cycle EMI #6 -> #7. The database matched: two new Late repayment events (59 and 46 days), reduced loan balances, and score rows noted "Recomputed after live repayment". The server log had no 500s -- the only 401/403s were the app's own pre-login session check and my deliberate wrong-owner / no-token tests -- and the database was restored to its starting state (wallet 14,272.50, 16,993 events) at the end.

    Scope of that run: one borrower through the UI (plus one more for the insufficient-funds case), not the whole population.

    Feature: a guided demo inside the app

    To present the system without clicking through four logins by hand, I added an in-app guided demo, modelled on the one in my RL autoscaler project. A "Guided demo" button in the navigation (and on the home page) opens a choice of five tours:

    Tour Steps Changes data?
    End-to-end: loan request to repayment (all four roles) 16 Yes: 1 request, 1 loan, 1 payment
    Borrower 15 Yes: 1 request (then withdrawn), 1 payment
    Lender 11 No
    Self-help group 7 No
    Bank / admin 10 No

    The tour drives the real app rather than a recording: it logs in through the real login form, opens pages, types and clicks, highlights the element each step is about, and explains it in a panel at the bottom. The panel has a step counter, a list to jump to any step, Previous / Next, auto-play that moves on when the narration ends, spoken narration through the browser's speech engine (with a voice picker that prefers a natural male English voice), Hide text and Close.

    Three design decisions mattered:

    • Demo accounts are discovered, not hard-coded. Phone numbers change every time the data is reseeded, so the tour signs in as the admin behind the scenes and picks an SHG member who has a payable EMI and an eligible lender, plus that lender and the member's SHG, and checks those logins work before using them.
    • Data-changing steps run once per tour. Going back with Previous shows the earlier result again but never repeats a request, approval or payment. Each step marks itself done just before its irreversible click, so even a step that fails afterwards is not repeated. I checked this against the database: jumping back to the payment step left exactly one repayment.
    • Navigation is locked while a step is still working, so skipping ahead cannot interrupt a payment halfway.

    I ran every tour step by step in the browser: all 16 + 15 + 11 + 7 + 10 steps passed, auto-play advanced on its own, and narration started when the voice was turned on. The database was restored afterwards.

    Bug found by the guided demo: a paid-off loan vanished

    The borrower tour's payment step timed out on its first run, even though the payment had gone through. The payment closed the loan (its balance reached zero), the Repay page reloaded the due list, the closed loan dropped out of it, and its row disappeared together with the "Paid" confirmation. A real borrower paying off a loan would have seen it simply vanish. The page now keeps each paid row on screen with its result and says when the loan is fully repaid and closed.

    What I'd flag

    • About 68% of borrowers see no change at all from a single extra On_Time payment: one event is too small to cross a split in a model trained on ~475 people. That is a data-size limit, not a bug.
    • The remaining Late-above-On_Time cases (5%) are mostly legitimate: a late payment of fewer days than the borrower's average lowers their avg_days_late.
    • Holdout AUC on the single 95-person split dropped 0.910 -> 0.900; the cross-validated numbers show no real difference.
    • Fixed afterwards: lender_name came back null for every loan in the due-summary, because the generator deliberately seeded all ~1,650 historical loans with no lender ("pre-marketplace history"), so the repay page couldn't say who was being repaid. Backfilled by migrate_loan_lenders.py following the marketplace's own rules (an Approved-linked lender for SHG members, an independents-serving one for independents; deterministic by loan id, idempotent, live-flow loans untouched, 0 rule violations checked in SQL) and the generator now does the same. 38 loans whose SHG has no approved lender stay unattributed and show "Lender not recorded" instead of a blank.
    • The mock payment gateway's abc/123 credentials are hard-coded in the frontend -- fine for a demo, not for anything shown outside one.
